The response rule used here (T1, a teaching assumption): after the supply AVDD rises through 3.0 V, look for a new POR rise through 0.9 V within the next 2 ms, while AVDD stays above 3.0 V. The deadline is relative to that supply event. T1 is not the original designer’s established acceptance specification.

Read the graph and distinguish the three questions

AVDD is the rising supply voltage; osc_ck is the oscillator clock; POR is the power-on-reset output. A POR rise asserts reset (asks the circuit to reset); release happens later. We only study the start, not the full reset pulse or recovery from a later supply dip.

Delay measurement: in the selected window, find the first oscillator rise and first POR rise through the chosen voltage. The POR event must come after the oscillator event. Subtract their times. Defaults: start at 1.5 ms, use 0.9 V. A missing or reversed pair gives no valid delay.

Numerical delay limits (scalar limits): if a delay exists, compare it with the selected range. Response under T1: separately check the supply-to-POR rule above. The oscillator-to-POR delay and the supply-to-POR deadline have different starting events.

Horizontal axis = time in milliseconds (ms); vertical axis = voltage in volts (V). 1 ms = 0.001 s. A rising crossing is where a signal goes upward through a chosen voltage. Gray space after the red observation-end line is unseen data, not zero voltage. Dotted/dashed lines mark events; the legend names them.

HVL names the high-voltage cell-model library used in the simulator. “Old” and “corrected” refer to two recorded library versions. A known upstream level-shifter fix belongs to its original authors. DC override is a testbench supply setting; changing it is one controlled experiment. A simplified resistor changes a model assumption, not the physical fabricated circuit.
